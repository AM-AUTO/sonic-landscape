# Tae Be Walkit: the narrative

> Status: **draft for review.** Nothing in the levels changes until this is signed off.
> All copy below is placeholder in the game's Scots register, there to be rewritten.

## The spine: the Cailleach's winter

The album is one winter, from Samhain (1 November) to Bealltainn (1 May).

The Cailleach is the old woman of winter. She built these hills: the stones fell from the creel on her back as she strode over the country. She washes her plaid in the whirlpool of Corryvreckan, and when she spreads it on the hills to dry, the snow lies. Every pass is held in her frost. She herds the deer on the high ground. Through the dark half of the year she keeps Brìghde, the spring, shut inside Ben Nevis. At Bealltainn she lets go: she throws her staff down under a holly bush, where no grass grows, and becomes a grey stone until Samhain comes round again.

**The walker** has no name and no quest beyond getting home before the light comes back. They are not a hero and they do not fight her. They walk her country while it is hers, and the land lets them through when they hear it. Waking a place's chain of songs thaws its bealach.

**The ending:** spring comes out of the mountain. The summit lifts a whole tone into lydian, which the Nevis profile already does, and now it has a reason. The standing stone on the title screen is her, asleep until Samhain. The game starts and ends at that stone.

### Why this spine
- **Place.** Her legends sit on specific places, so the route follows the myth rather than the other way round.
- **Look.** The winter look is already where the art has gone: bleak moor, snow, greys, with blue as the second tonal ramp.
- **Stones.** It explains the stones everywhere: cairns, erratics, standing stones and the title screen.
- **Time.** It gives time of day and weather meaning. Days shorten to the solstice and lengthen to Bealltainn, so resting through a long midwinter night is a real choice.
- **Clarity.** It turns the abstract gate chains into small local rites the player can reason about. This answers the opacity in the first playtest: the legend *is* the hint.

## The musical ethos: brightness follows the season

- The roots keep the album's circle-of-fifths walk **by slot**, D G A E C F♯ E♭ D, and come home to D.
- New: **the mode tracks the light.** Modes darken from Samhain to the solstice and brighten to Bealltainn. The album is a V on the brightness scale (lydian > ionian > mixolydian > dorian > aeolian > phrygian > locrian).

| Slot | Place | Root | Mode (dawn / night variants darker or lighter by one step) | Pulse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Corryvreckan & Jura | D | dorian | 64 |
| II | Ben Cruachan & Loch Awe | G | aeolian | 66 |
| III | Glen Lyon | A | aeolian, phrygian at night | 60 |
| IV | Rannoch & Beinn a' Bhric | E | phrygian | 58 |
| V | Cairngorm Plateau | C | octatonic / locrian. Suspended, the slowest pulse | 52 |
| VI | Glen Coe | F♯ | phrygian → aeolian. The storm | 76 |
| VII | The Cuillin | E♭ | dorian → mixolydian | 72 |
| VIII | Ben Nevis | D | ionian → lydian at the summit | 70 |

Each existing profile keeps its character (Rannoch's glass bells, Cairngorm's frozen grains, Coe's toms and gong, Cuillin's metallic glitch). Only its root and modal colour move. The three new profiles grow from the nearest old ones:
- Assynt's stillness and long delays → Corryvreckan's sea
- Affric's falling water → Cruachan
- Torridon's terraced pulse → Glen Lyon's long glen

**Her voice.** One low, wind-like drone figure belongs to the Cailleach across the whole album:
- heard faintly at Samhain
- strongest on the solstice plateau
- thinning through Imbolc
- absent on Nevis after the summit wakes

She is heard, never shown.

## The season engine (what it changes in play)

Each level carries a season:
- **daylight share:** day, dawn and dusk against night
- **start time**
- **snow line**

Time conditions on POIs mean more as a result. A dawn rite at midwinter needs a long wait, or a rest through the night at ×8.

| Slot | Date | Night share of the day cycle | Snow line (m) | Weather |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Samhain | 0.55 | ~600, the Paps of Jura only. Snow arrives when the gate opens | sea fret, wind, the whirlpool's roar |
| II | late November | 0.60 | ~850 | rain turning to sleet, cloud down on Cruachan |
| III | December | 0.63 | ~650 | hard frost, still air, stars |
| IV | midwinter | 0.66 | ~450, the moor white | snow showers, deer on the skyline |
| V | the solstice | 0.70 | everything on the plateau | white-out by day, the merry dancers by night |
| VI | the wolf-month (January) | 0.62 | ~800 | storm, lightning in the glen |
| VII | Imbolc (1 February) | 0.50 | ~750 | clearing, long low light on the sea, last showers |
| VIII | Bealltainn (1 May) | 0.30 | ~1100, gully snow only | thaw, burns in spate, a clear dawn |

**The thaw as a visible reward.** When a chain completes, the snow around the bealach melts back on the map as the gate opens. Over the album the snow line drops to the solstice and then retreats.

## The route

Each **rite** below is the level's gate chain, written in the existing condition grammar (`speed`, `approach`, `climb`, `dwell`, `time`, `requires`). There is no new mechanic and the eight artefact types are kept, so knowledge still carries from level to level. Names marked * are invented descriptive Gaelic, to be checked.

### I. Corryvreckan & Jura: Samhain *(the teaching level)*
- **The legend.** On the first day of winter the Cailleach washes her plaid in the Corryvreckan. For three days the roar of it carries for miles. When she lifts it out it is white, and she spreads it on the hills.
- **The land.** The north end of Jura: raised beaches, caves, bog, the Paps rising inland, and across the gulf, Scarba. The whirlpool is a map feature, shown with the OS tidal-race symbol and labelled *Coire Bhreacain*. It is a sound landmark: a granular roar that swells and falls on a slow tide. It is not an artefact.
- **The rite** (2 links, the old Rannoch lessons):
  - a lochan, approached slowly
  - a cairn on the shoulder of the Paps, approached from above
- **The bealach.** The way off the island. When it opens, the first snow falls: weather changes from fret to snow.
- **Field-note seeds:** the roar heard from the bothy at night; the plaid; the Paps going white; "come canny, an come doun tae it".

### II. Ben Cruachan & Loch Awe: late November
- **The legend.** She kept a spring on the summit. Every sunset she laid a slab over it, and every sunrise she lifted it off. One night she slept. The water burst out, ran down through the Pass of Brander and drowned the glen, and that is Loch Awe.
- **The land.** The Cruachan horseshoe above a long freshwater loch. The Pass of Brander is a gorge with the river in it.
- **The rite** (teaches time):
  - the summit at **dusk**: cap the well
  - the waterfall in the Pass at **dawn**: let it go
  - Between them, the player learns to rest through a long night.
- **Field-note seeds:** the slab; the night she slept; the drowned townships; a bothy book entry about waiting for light.

### III. Glen Lyon: December
- **The legend.** Two strangers, a huge man and woman, came to the glen in a snowstorm. The people took them in. In thanks they left the glen fertile while their stone family is cared for. The stones are put out of their wee house at Bealltainn and brought in at Samhain, and someone still does it.
- **The land.** The longest enclosed glen in Scotland: narrow, steep-sided, old pine, a high side glen (Gleann Cailliche) with a shieling house. Schiehallion is seen on the skyline.
- **The rite:**
  - the stones, by **dwelling** with them
  - the house at **dusk**: shutting them in for the night. The house is the bothy type.
- **Field-note seeds:** the stones by size; the snowstorm; "naebody kens wha keeps them, but they're aye keepit".

### IV. Rannoch Moor & Beinn a' Bhric: midwinter
- **The legend.** The Cailleach of Beinn a' Bhric herds the hinds on the hill above Loch Treig and milks them like cattle. A hunter who kills a deer of hers pays for it.
- **The land.** The current Rannoch sheet: lochans, the old track, Ba Bothy, granite erratics, with the Beinn a' Bhric ground at its north edge.
- **The rite:**
  - a lochan, slowly
  - an erratic at **night**
  - a cairn, from above
- **Field-note seeds:** deer on the skyline at dusk; the milking song; the stone the ice set down.

### V. The Cairngorm Plateau: the solstice
- **The legend.** The high ground is hers more than anywhere. On the longest night the merry dancers play over the plateau.
- **The land.** The current Cairngorm sheet, including Clach Dhìon, the Shelter Stone (a nod to the shelter start screen), and the Làirig Ghrù.
- **The rite** (wait out the longest night, then meet the sun's return):
  - the summit, by **climbing**
  - a cairn
  - a lochan at **dawn**
- **Field-note seeds:** the Shelter Stone; the white-out; the dancers; "the langest nicht. Bide it oot."

### VI. Glen Coe: the wolf-month
- **The legend.** The storms of the Faoilleach, the wolf-month, are hers. The glen is also where the Fianna hunted: Sgòr nam Fiannaidh and Ossian's Cave above the glen. That is a nod only; the Fianna are not a second spine.
- **The land.** The current Coe sheet.
- **The rite** (kept; it already reads as a storm):
  - a crag at **speed**
  - a stone, by **dwelling**
  - the trig, by **climbing**
- **Field-note seeds:** lightning on the Aonach Eagach; the Fianna's peak; a winter ascent abandoned.

### VII. The Cuillin: Imbolc
- **The legend.** On Brìghde's day the Cailleach goes out for firewood. If the day is bright she gathers plenty, and winter will last. If it is foul she sleeps, and winter is nearly done. Beinn na Caillich, with its huge cairn, stands at the edge of the Red Cuillin.
- **The land.** The current Cuillin sheet, extended to take in the Red Hills and Beinn na Caillich.
- **The rite:**
  - a crag at **speed**
  - the cairn on Beinn na Caillich, from **above**
  - a lochan, slowly, **while the cairn still resonates**
  - a stone, by **dwelling**
- **Field-note seeds:** the cairn; Brìghde's day; the compass the gabbro won't let you trust.

### VIII. Ben Nevis: Bealltainn
- **The legend.** Spring has been shut inside the mountain all winter. At Bealltainn she comes out. The Cailleach throws her staff under a holly bush and turns to stone.
- **The land.** The current Nevis sheet in thaw, with Steall in spate.
- **The rite** (kept):
  - the waterfall
  - the hut
  - the buttress at **speed**
  - the summit, which requires every fragment and the trig
- **The summit.** The key lifts into lydian.
- **After.** No cutscene. The last thing the player sees is the map, green coming through. Back at the title, the stone.
- **Field-note seeds:** the observatory men; snow in the gullies in May; "hame".

## What the narrative opens up for level design (possibilities, not commitments)
- **Rites from legends.** Every chain has a reason the player can guess from the place's story. The field notes become the telling, and the hints become the legend's own words.
- **Daylight as a resource.** The long nights midwinter and the short nights at Bealltainn change how a level plays, with no new rules.
- **Her presence as sound.** One recurring voice that the album slowly takes away.
- **The thaw.** Snow retreats around each opened bealach, and across the album.
- **Deer.** A herd that moves off if you run. Silent at first; later a moving sound source. Only if it earns its keep.
- **Coming back to the parked between-track beats.** If any return, they would be her: one still image of the stone and one line per track, never a filmstrip. See `docs/PARKED.md`.

## Copy rules
- Short. No strapline, no lore dump, no "ancient" or "mystical".
- The legend is told in plain words, in the bothy books and the hints. The game never explains itself.
- The user rewrites the final Scots.
